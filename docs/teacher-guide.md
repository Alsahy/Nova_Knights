# Mars Base Adventure: teacher guide

*One page for running the game with a class of 7–12-year-olds. Fan-made educational game. Not affiliated with or endorsed by NASA. Uses public NASA data.*

## What it is
Students lead a small Mars base with 4 astronauts for 10 days ("sols"). Each sol starts with a surprise: they pick one of two cards, then give each astronaut a job. Every choice costs something. To win, they must send **14 science stars** to Earth **and** keep the crew's radiation badge out of the red. One game takes about 10–15 minutes. No login, nothing to install, no data collected.

## Running it in class (projector)
1. Open the game link on the classroom computer. In **Settings**, turn on **Projector mode** (bigger text). For a class vote, set the **surprise timer** to **20 seconds** or **Off**.
2. Play the guided first day together (**Tutorial** on the title screen).
3. For each surprise, read the card out loud and let the class vote with hands up: card 1 or card 2. Hover over or tap a card to see which bars it changes.
4. Let a different student pick each astronaut's job.
5. At the end, open **"Your mission, day by day"** and talk about the biggest choices.

## Three discussion questions
1. **Shield or science?** When the Sun flared, did you keep everyone inside (safe, but no science) or keep working? What would a real mission planner do?
2. **Why send science home?** Stars only count once they reach Earth, and messages take 3 to 22 minutes each way. What happens to an experiment you never share?
3. **What did "doing nothing" cost?** When the timer ran out, the crew chose on its own. Why do real crews plan before trouble arrives?

## The NASA science behind the 14 surprises
| Surprise | What it teaches | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Flash on the Sun! | Flares come in classes A, B, C, M, X; each is 10× stronger. *Real record: X3.9 flare, 10 May 2024* | [S3] [S4], NASA DONKI |
| Storm Cloud Coming / The Storm Cloud Hits | NASA's model predicts when a solar cloud (CME) reaches Mars. *Real model run: arrival 13 May 2024* | [S2], NASA DONKI |
| Radiation Storm! | Particles arrive fast; shelters and water walls protect crews. Curiosity measured 8,100 µGy in May 2024 | [S4] [S6] [S10] [S9] |
| Quiet Sun Day | Even on calm days, Mars' surface gets about 0.64 mSv a day | [S8] |
| Hot Machine! | Spacecraft carry heat away with liquid cooling loops | [S29] |
| Dusty Solar Panels / Lucky Wind! | Dust blocked 75 % of Spirit's sunlight; a wind cleaned it in 2009 | [S17] |
| Freezing Night | Viking 1 measured −89 °C to −31 °C | [S13] |
| Stuffy Air | Each astronaut breathes out about 1 kg of CO₂ a day | [S21] [S22] |
| Oxygen Maker Stopped | People need about 0.8 kg of oxygen a day; backups have risks | [S21] [S25] |
| Water Recycler Clogged | The ISS recycles sweat and urine into drinking water (98 %) | [S27] |
| Shiny Rock Far Away | Spacewalks are limited by suit air (about 6.5 hours) | [S47] |
| Send Your Data Home | Signals take 3–22 minutes between Mars and Earth | [S54] [S44] |

The full source list is on the in-game screen **"Where our data comes from"**. Numbers on cards are game values: slow processes run 15× faster, and storms are made bigger than real ones so shields matter.
